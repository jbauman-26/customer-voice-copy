# Line rules and first-message rules

Summaries in our own words of two talks. The timestamps point to where each idea comes up, so
you can watch the source yourself. They are provenance for the ideas, not proof of results.

- Jay Yang, copywriting masterclass: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OP_eMk_RCWg
- Harry Dry (Marketing Examples), interview on writing: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TUMjnmfsPeM
  The interview is about ads, headlines and newsletters. Where a rule is applied to outreach it
  is marked [applied].

## From Jay Yang (22:01 onward)

| Rule | Time |
|---|---|
| Price every word at $10. Before publishing ask what can be cut without losing meaning | 22:01 |
| Make it picturable. Swap the general claim for the specific case with its numbers | 23:33 |
| When the subject is large and vague, zoom in on one moment | 23:33 |
| Use the reader's own words and register. Read it aloud; if nobody talks that way, rewrite | 24:19 |
| Vary sentence length. Read aloud and squint at the shape of the page | 25:04 to 26:36 |
| The copy is a shop window: the reader should see the offer, not the writing | 27:21 |
| Hook score: benefit x relevance x credibility, divided by perceived effort | 28:07 |
| Teaching body: what, why, how. Story body: but, therefore | 29:40, 30:25 |
| CTA: who, what, when, how. One CTA, one reader, one offer | 31:10 |
| Value comes from three levers: save time, make money, reduce risk | 35:44 |
| When they say no, check three things: is it hard to say yes, did I show how it pays, do they doubt I can do it | 35:44 to 36:29 |
| With no case studies, say so and offer to do the work first | 37:15 |
| Show you want the work and can do it by doing the work up front | 38:01 to 38:48 |
| Target: pick one person or company whose direction you know | 39:34 |
| Audit: research them until you can answer "what would I do first in this job", then go through the asset top to bottom | 39:34 to 41:05 |
| Gift: turn the audit into something they can read in a minute on a phone, plus ready-to-use work they can test immediately | 41:05 to 41:52 |
| Match the form of the gift to the person (scrappy for someone who values scrappy) | 41:05 |

|---|---|

## Applied to a first message [applied]

- A first line passes when it would be false with another prospect's name in it.
- It names the page, the button or the number, not "your marketing".
- It is a fact first, then what it means for them.
- The sender's credentials do not pass the "nobody else could say it" test when they are a job
  history. A specific finding about the reader does.
