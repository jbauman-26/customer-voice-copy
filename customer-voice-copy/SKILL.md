# Customer Voice Copy

A copywriting skill built by Jake Bauman. The 6 Ps, the 5 Cs and the hook score come from
Jay Yang's copywriting teaching. The line-level tests come from Harry Dry (Marketing Examples).
The audience and offer thinking leans on Seth Godin (smallest viable audience) and Alex
Hormozi (the value equation). Everything here is written in our own words.

**The first rule:** good copy is a thinking problem, not a writing problem. Claude can write
fluent sentences about anyone. It can only write sentences that land when it knows who the
reader is, what hurts, and the words they use for it. So do not draft until you have more
material than you need.

## Step 0: Pick the mode

| The job | Mode | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| Caption, tweet, single hook, one line to rewrite | **Light** | 6 Ps in one line each, write, then the 5 Cs |
| Long post, newsletter, email, script, landing page, outreach | **Full** | Fill the 6 Ps worksheet below, then write |
| Ads | **Ads** | Full mode, plus write 5 angles and keep the body to about 180 to 210 words |
| An offer or a pitch | **Offer** | Full mode, plus the value equation in Step 1 |

State the mode in one line, then go.

## Step 1: Map the reader (empathy map)

Before the 6 Ps, fill four boxes about one reader, in their words where you can:

| Box | Question |
|---|---|
| Today | What does their normal day look like? What do they see, hear and deal with? |
| Frustrations | What annoys them right now? What do they keep trying to avoid? |
| Fears | Where do they not want to end up? What is the worst version of the next year? |
| Dreams | Where do they want to be? What would they brag about if it worked? |

**Go and get the material yourself. Do not make the user do the research.** In this order:
1. Look in what you can already reach: files in the project, connected tools (Shopify,
   Klaviyo, a helpdesk, Google Drive), and anything pasted earlier in the chat.
2. Search the open web for pain points. If Firecrawl is connected (MCP or CLI), use it:
   `firecrawl_search` for the category's problems on Reddit, community forums, review sites
   and Q&A threads (for example "weighted sleep mask" plus words like "problem", "hate",
   "finally", "wish"), then scrape the best threads and review pages in full. Without
   Firecrawl, use whatever web search and fetch you have. Read the brand's own site and
   product pages too.
3. Count what repeats. Group the complaints and wishes into pain points, note how many
   times each came up and where, and keep the best exact quotes for each.
4. Tell the user in one line what you are pulling and from where, then do it.
5. Only ask the user for something you could not find, and say what you found first.

Copy their exact phrases into the boxes, with where each came from. When a box is still
empty after you looked, mark it `[GAP]`. Do not draft until you have more to say than you need. The pain, said their
way, becomes the hook. The dream becomes the promise.

## Step 1b: Think (the 6 Ps)

Answer each in one line from real material: customer reviews, support tickets, sales calls,
comments, survey answers, the offer itself. Use what you gathered in Step 1.

| P | Question | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| People | Who exactly is this for? | One person you can picture, described in their own words |
| Positioning | Why you over anyone else? | Be first to say a true fact the others skip |
| Promise | What do they get? | Ask "so what?" until the answer is about their life |
| Proof | Why believe it? | Proof the reader trusts, placed next to the claim it supports |
| Priority | Why now? | The cost of waiting beats hype. Scarcity only if it is true |
| Process | How does it work? | "Everyone thinks X, the real problem is Y, so the fix is Z" |

Then choose:
- **One belief** this piece moves. One piece, one belief.
- **Awareness level.** Unaware: name the problem. Problem aware: show the solution. Solution
  aware: say why yours. Product aware: prove it. Most aware: make the offer.
- **Smallest viable audience.** The narrowest group that would miss this if it were gone.
- For offers, the **value equation**: dream outcome and the reader's confidence they will get
  it, against the time it takes and the effort it costs. Cut time and effort before you promise
  a bigger outcome.

Mark gaps instead of filling them: `[NEEDS NUMBER]`, `[NEEDS STORY]`, `[GAP: what is missing]`.
Never invent a number, a quote, a testimonial or a result.

## Step 2: Write

**Hook first.** Its only job is getting the next line read.
- Write at least 5 hooks. Score each 1 to 5 on Benefit, Relevance and Credibility, and 1 to 5
  on Perceived Effort. Score = Benefit x Relevance x Credibility / Effort. Keep the top one.
- A hook is a promise plus an open loop, not a closed statement.
- Call out the reader: "If you're a [person] who [situation]...".
- Write a moment they lived, not a thesis. Numbers, names, things you can see.
- Pull hooks from the reader's own words. Their pain, said their way, is the pitch.
- Formats to rotate: call-out, "someone asked me", one hard line, story open, question, list.

**Body.** One belief, one reader.
- Teaching: **What, Why, How.**
- Story: **But, Therefore.** Never "and then".
- Every line needs a job: stakes, curiosity, a reason, proof or urgency. No job, cut it.

**CTA.** One call to action, one reader, one offer. Say who, what, when and how, and what
they get.

## Step 3: Edit, in this order

1. **Clear beats clever.** Cut any line that shows off instead of explaining.
2. **The three-question line test** for every line that makes a claim: Can they picture it?
   Can it be proved true or false? Could nobody else say it? Fails all three: cut it. Fails
   one: rewrite it. Then swap in a competitor's name. If the line still works, it is not
   specific yet.
3. **The 5 Cs.**
   - Clear: a tired person reading on a phone gets it. Aim for a grade 6 reading level.
   - Concise: pretend every word costs $10. Try cutting a third.
   - Concrete: they can picture it.
   - Conversational: the reader would say it this way. Read it out loud.
   - Cadence: mix short and long sentences. Squint at the page. A solid block means rewrite.
4. **Voice pass.** Rewrite any line the speaker would never say out loud.
5. **Kill pass.** Remove these on sight:
   - "It's not X, it's Y" and "not only X, but also Y"
   - Lists of three that exist only for rhythm
   - Summary one-liners and fake profound endings
   - Intensifiers (really, very, incredibly) and exaggeration
   - Em dashes. Use a comma, a period or parentheses.
   - Words that read as AI: delve, tapestry, landscape, leverage, unlock, seamless, robust,
     elevate, game-changer, "in today's world", "it's worth noting".
6. **Compliance.** No guaranteed income or results. Add a disclaimer wherever income, health
   or lifestyle results come up.

## Static ads: from copy to a finished image

When the job is a static ad, finish the copy (headline, sub-line, CTA, one line on the visual),
then turn it into an image brief: the scene, the exact text to render, the brand colors and
type, and a list of things to avoid (logos you don't own, made-up handles or URLs, robot or
glowing AI imagery). If an image tool is connected (GPT Image, the Graphed static-ads skill,
Midjourney or similar), generate the ad at 4:5 (1080x1350) and check the render: every word
spelled exactly as written, no invented logos or handles. If no image tool is connected,
hand over the brief ready to paste.

## First messages and cold outreach

Lead with them, not you. See [references/line-rules.md](references/line-rules.md).
1. **Audit first.** Go through their site and marketing until you can answer: if this were my
   job, what would I fix first? No audit, no message.
2. **Open with the finding.** One specific thing on their own site, stated as a fact they can
   check, with numbers exactly as their page shows them.
3. **Then the fix**, small enough to do today without you.
4. **Then a gift**: work already done they can use without a call.
5. **You get one sentence, or none**, after the gift. The first two sentences contain no
   "I", "we" or "my", except "I noticed".
6. **One ask, answerable in one word.**
7. **Banned openers:** a compliment, "I came across", "I help", "my name is", "hope this
   finds you well", or anything still true with another company's name in it.

Self-check: count the sentences about them and the sentences about you. More than one about
you means rewrite.

## Step 4: Deliver

Give the copy first, ready to paste. Under it, briefly:
- The 6 Ps in one line each, or just the gaps in Light mode.
- 2 to 4 alternate hooks with their scores.
- Every claim matched to its proof, and any `[NEEDS NUMBER]` the writer must fill.

## Keep getting better

When a piece performs, or when someone edits it, write down what won, what changed and the rule
it implies. Add that rule to your own voice notes so the next piece starts smarter.
